# Evidence for each rule

The research behind each rule in the `quiz` skill. Almost all studies use students, not professional engineers, so each rule is an extrapolation.

Strength: **Strong** = meta-analysis or replicated; **Moderate** = several primary studies or one large study; **Weak** = single small study, preprint, or secondary-source figure; **Unverified** = figure not checked against the primary text.

| Technique | Source | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Retrieval practice vs restudy | Rowland 2014, *Psych. Bull.* ([link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25150680/)) | g = 0.50 [0.42, 0.58], 159 ES. Strong |
| Retrieval practice in classrooms | Yang et al. 2021, *Psych. Bull.* ([link](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/testing-quizzing-boosts-classroom-learning-a-systematic-and-meta-/)) | g = 0.499, 222 studies, >48k students; moderated by feedback and format match. Strong |
| Recall vs recognition questions | Rowland 2014 ([link](https://courseware.epfl.ch/assets/courseware/v1/fdde2f0aa590bf3b1324077a6bf1540c/asset-v1%3AEPFL%2BDEMO%2B2020%2Btype%40asset%2Bblock/Rowland2014-meta-analysis.pdf)) | Cued recall g = 0.61 vs recognition g = 0.29 (high-exposure: 0.72/0.81 vs 0.36). Strong |
| MC as best practice format (conflicting) | Adesope et al. 2017, *RER* ([link](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316689306)) | MC most effective single format; likely driven by simple materials. Unverified |
| Transfer of testing to new questions | Pan & Rickard 2018, *Psych. Bull.* ([link](https://sc-pan.github.io/pdf/PR_2018.pdf)) | d = 0.40 overall; application/inference d = 0.32; ≈0 after bias correction without moderators. Strong |
| Elaborated retrieval + answer overlap | Pan & Rickard 2018 ([link](https://sc-pan.github.io/pdf/PR_2018.pdf)) | Both moderators present: d = 0.78; neither: d = 0.21. Moderate |
| Fact vs higher-order vs mixed quizzes | Agarwal 2019, *J. Educ. Psych.* ([link](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2019/6/27-1)) | Fact quizzes do not improve higher-order tests; mixed best on both. Moderate |
| Self-explanation / "why" prompts | Bisra et al. 2018, *Educ. Psych. Rev.* ([link](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2018-bisra.pdf)) | g = 0.55 overall; interrogative prompts g = 0.56. Strong |
| Explain in plain English + SOLO | Lister et al. 2006, ITiCSE ([link](https://www.cs.auckland.ac.nz/courses/compsci747s2c/lectures/p118-lister.pdf)) | Novices give multistructural, experts relational; relational predicts exam score. Moderate |
| Tracing and explaining predict writing | Lopez et al. 2008, ICER ([link](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/10806/1/2008001530.pdf)) | Together explain 46% of writing variance; N = 38, correlational. Weak–Moderate |
| Textbase vs situation model | McNamara et al. 1996 ([link](https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci1401_1)); Pennington 1987 ([link](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0285(87)90007-7)) | Text-based and inference measures diverge; function and data-flow knowledge form later. Moderate, unverified |
| Self-rated code understanding | Wyrich, Merz & Graziotin 2022, ICPC ([link](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.13705)) | Subjective ratings anchored by prior difficulty info. Weak |
| Number of MC options | Rodriguez 2005, *EM:IP* ([link](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-3992.2005.00006.x)) | 3 options generally optimal. Strong, unverified |
| Two-tier MCQ | Treagust 1988, *IJSE* ([link](https://doi.org/10.1080/0950069880100204)) | Reason tier separates right-for-wrong-reason. Moderate, unverified |
| Feedback overall and by type | Wisniewski, Zierer & Hattie 2020 ([link](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6987456)) | d = 0.48; high-information d = 0.99; praise/reward d = 0.24. Strong |
| Feedback can backfire | Kluger & DeNisi 1996, *Psych. Bull.* ([link](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.119.2.254)) | >1/3 of effects negative; self-focused feedback weakens effects. Strong |
| Elaborated vs KCR vs KR | Van der Kleij et al. 2015, *RER* ([link](https://djplaner.github.io/memex/sense/Learning/learning-research/research-feedback/)) | EF ≈ 0.49, KCR ≈ 0.32, KR ≈ 0.05; EF best for higher-order outcomes. Moderate, unverified means |
| Right/wrong-only feedback | Pashler et al. 2005, *JEP:LMC* ([link](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.31.1.3)) | No benefit over no feedback; correct-answer feedback large gain on errors. Moderate |
| Commit before feedback | Bangert-Drowns et al. 1991, *RER* ([link](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543061002213)) | Feedback weaker when answer is available before responding. Moderate |
| Delayed feedback for transfer | Mullet et al. 2014, *JARMAC* ([link](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1051154)) | 1-week delay improved transfer in an engineering course; students preferred immediate. Moderate |
| Immediate feedback in applied settings | Kulik & Kulik 1988, *RER* ([link](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543058001079)) | Applied studies favour immediate; lab list studies favour delay. Moderate, unverified |
| Hypercorrection | Butterfield & Metcalfe 2001 ([link](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.27.6.1491)); Metcalfe 2017 ([link](https://annualreviews.org/doi/abs/10.1146/annurev-psych-010416-044022)) | High-confidence errors corrected more often; trivia-based evidence. Moderate |
| Feedback on low-confidence correct answers | Butler, Karpicke & Roediger 2008 ([link](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/correcting-a-metacognitive-error-feedback-increases-retention-of-/)) | Feedback improves retention of tentative correct answers (≈2x, unverified). Moderate |
| Refutation feedback | Schroeder & Kucera 2022 ([link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-021-09656-z)); Danielson et al. 2024/25 ([link](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10629626)) | g = 0.41, 44 comparisons; preregistered MA confirms advantage. Strong for texts, extrapolated to feedback |
| Multiple-try with hints | Attali 2015, *Comp. & Educ.* ([link](https://www.ets.org/research/policy_research_reports/publications/article/2015/jwdp.html)) | Hinted retry > retry > KCR; open-ended > MC. Moderate |
| Answer-until-correct without hints | Butler, Karpicke & Roediger 2007 ([link](https://pcllab.github.io/downloads/2007/2007_Butler_Karpicke_Roediger_JEPA.pdf)) | Equivalent to showing the correct answer. Moderate |
| Hints raise retrieval uptake | Vaughn & Kornell 2019, *CRPI* ([link](https://cognitiveresearchjournal.springeropen.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s41235-019-0187-y)) | Hints make retrieval chosen more; small hints ≈ pure retrieval; giveaway hints hurt. Moderate |
| Praise-for-effort | Li & Bates 2019 ([link](https://mrbartonmaths.com/resourcesnew/8.%20Research/Mindset/Mindset%20replication.pdf)) | Replication found no effect on 8 motivation measures. Contested |
| Guardrailed vs plain LLM tutor | Bastani et al. 2025, *PNAS* ([link](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v122y2025pe2422633122.html)) | Plain GPT: exam worse than control; hint-only tutor removed harm. Strong (one large RCT) |
| AI vs human feedback | Kaliisa et al. 2025 ([link](https://dev.edupedu.ro/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/How-does-artificial-intelligence-compare-to-human-feedback-A-meta-analysis-of-performance-feedback-perception-and-learning-dispositions.pdf)) | g = 0.25 [−0.11, 0.60], no significant difference. Moderate |
| Substitutive vs explanatory LLM use | Lehmann, Cornelius & Sting 2024/25 ([link](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09047v2)) | Explanation use helps; solving-for-you hurts; perceived > actual benefit. Weak–Moderate (preprint) |
| Validating LLM hints | Phung et al. 2024, LAK ([link](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03780v4)) | Raw GPT-4 hints ≈60% vs >90% human; validator raises precision to ≈95%. Moderate, figures not reconciled |
| Rubric conditions for reliability | Jonsson & Svingby 2007 ([link](https://www.koji.so/visuals/scoring-rubric-reliability-conditions)) | Analytic, task-specific, exemplars, rater training. Strong review, secondary source |
| Rubric design | Brookhart 2018, *Front. Educ.* ([link](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/feduc.2018.00022/full)) | 3–5 levels norm; criteria quality matters most; no analytic-vs-holistic reliability pattern. Strong (descriptive) |
| LLM short-answer grading | Henkel et al. 2024 ([link](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.02985)); Jiang & Bosch 2024 ([link](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3657604.3664685)) | GPT-4 κ = 0.70 vs human 0.75; QWK = 0.677. Moderate |
| Question-specific rubrics for code grading | Pathak et al. 2025, ICER ([link](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.23989v3)) | Specific > generic rubrics; ICC3 = 0.819. Moderate |
| CoT, few-shot, human-in-the-loop grading | BEA 2025 survey ([link](https://aclanthology.org/anthology-files/pdf/bea/2025.bea-1.35.pdf)) | HITL 0.90, CoT 0.81, zero-shot 0.53 (pooled metrics); temp 0 better. Directional only |
| LLM judge biases | Zheng et al. 2023, NeurIPS ([link](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685v4)) | >80% human agreement; position, verbosity, self-enhancement bias. Strong |
| LLM grading of partial answers | Schleifer et al. 2026, preprint ([link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07647)) | All models degrade on mid-range answers; humans stable. Weak |
| Grades vs comments | Koenka et al. 2021 ([link](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1427115)); Butler 1987 ([link](https://mrbartonmaths.com/resourcesnew/8.%20Research/Marking%20and%20Feedback/Task-Involving%20and%20Ego-Involving%20Properties%20of%20Evaluation.pdf)) | Comments beat grades for achievement and motivation; K-12 only. Strong for children |
| Mastery learning | Kulik, Kulik & Bangert-Drowns 1990, *RER* ([link](https://typeset.io/pdf/effectiveness-of-mastery-learning-programs-a-meta-analysis-45bgwh1byb.pdf)) | Positive effects, 108 evaluations; ≈0.52 SD (secondary); contested by Slavin. Moderate |
| Certainty-based marking | Smrkolj et al. 2022 ([link](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8834968/)); Gardner-Medwin ([link](https://www.physoc.org/abstracts/analysis-of-exams-using-certainty-based-marking/)) | No score difference, 94% found useful; reliability claims from abstracts. Weak |
| Calibration and overconfidence | Dunlosky & Rawson 2012, *L&I* ([link](https://core.ac.uk/download/42152364.pdf)) | Accurate judgments predict retention; overconfident learners stop early. Moderate |
| Confidence-prompt reactivity | Double, Birney & Walker 2018, *Memory* ([link](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/2253677/a-meta-analysis-and-systematic-review-of-reactivity-to-judgements-of-learning)) | g = 0.054, not significant; confidence prompts do not hurt memory. Strong |
| "85% rule" difficulty | Wilson et al. 2019, *Nat. Commun.* ([link](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6831579)) | ≈85% accuracy optimal for ML binary classifiers; not tested on humans with complex material. Weak for this use |
| Item difficulty ordering | Leary & Dorans review ([link](https://journalhosting.ucalgary.ca/index.php/ajer/article/view/55572/42529)) | No clear-cut conclusions. Weak |
| Interleaving | Brunmair & Richter 2019, *Psych. Bull.* ([link](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31556629/)) | g = 0.42; strong for similar categories, weak for expository text. Strong |
| Prequestions | Pan et al. 2024, *PB&R* ([link](https://www.ollielovell.com/how-you-can-use-prequestioning-to-boost-student-learning/)) | Specific g = 0.54 (k = 97); general effect ≈ 0. Unverified |
| Spaced vs massed retrieval | Latimier, Peyre & Ramus 2021, *Educ. Psych. Rev.* ([link](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10648-020-09572-8)) | g = 0.74, 29 studies. Strong |
| Successive relearning | Rawson et al. 2018 ([link](https://www.retrievalpractice.org/archive/2018/successive-relearning)) | 1 recall x 3 sessions: 68% vs 3 recalls x 1 session: 26% at 1 week. Moderate |
| Optimal spacing gap | Cepeda et al. 2008, *Psych. Sci.* ([link](https://www.evullab.org/pdf/CepedaVulRohrerWixtedPashler-PS-2008.pdf)) | Gap ≈20–40% of retention interval at 1 week, ≈5–10% at 1 year. Strong study, ratios unverified |
| Low-stakes framing and anxiety | Agarwal et al. 2014, *JARMAC* ([link](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/classroom-based-programs-of-retrieval-practice-reduce-middle-scho/)) | 72% of 1,408 students report less test nervousness; survey only. Weak |
| Simplified wording of test items | Abedi & Lord 2001, *Applied Measurement in Education* ([link](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15324818AME1403_2)) | Linguistically simplified math items raised scores of English learners and low performers; small effect; no harm to others. Moderate, unverified |
| ASD-STE100 | Chervak, Drury & Ouellette 1996; Shubert et al. 1995; Eckert 1997 | Better comprehension of aircraft workcards, mostly for difficult texts and non-native readers. Moderate |
